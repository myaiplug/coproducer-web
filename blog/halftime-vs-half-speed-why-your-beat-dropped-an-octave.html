Halftime vs half speed: why your halftime beat dropped an octave

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 06

Halftime and half speed both put the snare once a bar, but only half speed drops the whole beat an octave. Halftime is a drum pattern: the tempo stays, the snare moves to beat 3, and the hats, 808 and vocal keep their pitch. Half speed is tape-style slowing at 50%: in our test a 140 BPM beat ran twice as long, its 49 Hz 808 fell to 24.5 Hz, and the 16th-note hats slowed into 8ths. Same heavy pulse, very different record.

Somebody asks for a halftime version. You slow the beat to 50%, it sounds enormous for about four bars, and then you notice the hats have gone soft, the vocal sounds like a movie monster and the 808 is something you feel more than hear. That might be exactly what you wanted. It might also not be what they meant.

People use the word for two different moves. One is a way of programming drums. The other is a speed change. They share a feel, which is why they get mixed up, and they share almost nothing else.

Two things with one name

Halftime keeps the song at its tempo and changes the drums. The snare that used to land on 2 and 4 lands once a bar, on 3, and the kick usually opens up with it. The grid is still 140. Everything melodic stays where it was.

Half speed plays the whole file at 50%, the way a turntable or tape does. Tempo and pitch drop together, so every sound goes down exactly an octave and every bar lasts twice as long. That’s the screwed version of the idea, and it’s what a TIME knob at 50% does.

To see the difference in numbers, we built a two-bar beat at 140 BPM in halftime_check.py: a kick, a G1 808 at 49 Hz with a little drive so it has harmonics, a snare on 2 and 4, and 16th hats. Then we made it two ways. Halftime: the same sounds reprogrammed at 140 with the snare on 3. Half speed: the original run through HalfScrew’s resampler, ported from its source, at 50%. Each drum was rendered on its own so we could measure it cleanly.

# Halftime vs half speed: what each one does to a 140 BPM beat, measured on rendered stems.
# Half speed = HalfScrew's resampler ported from halfscrew.js resample() (v=44), ratio 0.5, WOW 0, 4-point Hermite (HQ).
# Halftime = the same sounds reprogrammed at the same 140 BPM: snare once a bar on beat 3, kick thinned, hats untouched.
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import butter, sosfilt
fs = 48000; rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
BPM = 140; beat = 60 / BPM; bars = 2; L = int(round(bars * 4 * beat * fs))

def hermite(y0, y1, y2, y3, f):
    c1 = 0.5 * (y2 - y0); c2 = y0 - 2.5 * y1 + 2 * y2 - 0.5 * y3; c3 = 0.5 * (y3 - y0) + 1.5 * (y1 - y2)
    return ((c3 * f + c2) * f + c1) * f + y1
def halfscrew_resample(a, ratio):
    n = int(np.ceil(len(a) / ratio)); pos = np.arange(n) * ratio; i0 = np.floor(pos).astype(int); f = pos - i0
    g = lambda k: np.where((k >= 0) & (k < len(a)), a[np.clip(k, 0, len(a) - 1)], 0.0)
    return hermite(g(i0 - 1), g(i0), g(i0 + 1), g(i0 + 2), f)

t = lambda sec: np.arange(int(sec * fs)) / fs
def place(hits, sound):
    y = np.zeros(L)
    for b in hits:
        i = int(round(b * beat * fs)); seg = sound[: L - i]; y[i:i + len(seg)] += seg
    return y
hp = lambda x, fc: sosfilt(butter(4, fc, 'highpass', fs=fs, output='sos'), x)
kick = np.sin(2 * np.pi * np.cumsum(49 + 120 * np.exp(-t(0.25) * 30)) / fs) * np.exp(-t(0.25) * 12)
sub808 = np.tanh(2.5 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 49 * t(1.6))) * np.exp(-t(1.6) * 1.5)   # G1 808, driven so it has harmonics
snare = (0.6 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 200 * t(0.2)) + hp(rng.standard_normal(int(0.2 * fs)), 1500) * 0.5) * np.exp(-t(0.2) * 22)
hat = sosfilt(butter(4, [6000, 12000], 'bandpass', fs=fs, output='sos'), rng.standard_normal(int(0.05 * fs))) * np.exp(-t(0.05) * 90) * 0.4

grid16 = [i / 4 for i in range(bars * 16)]
orig = {"kick": place([0, 2.5, 4, 6.5], kick), "808": place([0, 4], sub808),
        "snare": place([1, 3, 5, 7], snare), "hats": place(grid16, hat)}
halftime = {"kick": place([0, 4], kick), "808": place([0, 4], sub808),
            "snare": place([2, 6], snare), "hats": place(grid16, hat)}
halfspeed = {k: halfscrew_resample(v, 0.5) for k, v in orig.items()}

def onsets(y):
    k = int(0.005 * fs); env = np.sqrt(np.convolve(y ** 2, np.ones(k) / k, 'same'))
    above = env > 0.25 * env.max(); idx = np.flatnonzero(above[1:] & ~above[:-1]) + 1
    keep = [idx[0]]
    for i in idx[1:]:
        if i - keep[-1] > 0.06 * fs: keep.append(i)
    return np.array(keep) / fs
def spacing(y): o = onsets(y); return np.median(np.diff(o))
def centroid(y):
    S = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(y)) ** 2; f = np.fft.rfftfreq(len(y), 1 / fs); return (S * f).sum() / S.sum()
def peak_hz(y):
    S = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(y * np.hanning(len(y)))); f = np.fft.rfftfreq(len(y), 1 / fs); m = f > 15; return f[m][np.argmax(S[m])]
def below(y, fc):
    S = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(y)) ** 2; f = np.fft.rfftfreq(len(y), 1 / fs); return 100 * S[f < fc].sum() / S.sum()

print(f"Source: {bars}-bar beat at {BPM} BPM ({L/fs:.3f} s): kick, G1 808 (49 Hz), snare on 2 and 4, 16th hats\n")
print(f"{'':26}{'original':>12}{'halftime':>12}{'half speed':>12}")
rows = [("loop length (s)", lambda d: f"{len(d['hats'])/fs:.3f}"),
        ("snare to snare (s)", lambda d: f"{spacing(d['snare']):.3f}"),
        ("hat to hat (s)", lambda d: f"{spacing(d['hats']):.3f}"),
        ("808 note (Hz)", lambda d: f"{peak_hz(d['808']):.1f}"),
        ("808 energy below 30 Hz (%)", lambda d: f"{below(d['808'], 30):.0f}"),
        ("808 energy above 100 Hz (%)", lambda d: f"{below(d['808'], 1e9) - below(d['808'], 100):.1f}"),
        ("snare body (Hz)", lambda d: f"{peak_hz(sosfilt(butter(4, 1000, 'lowpass', fs=fs, output='sos'), d['snare'])):.0f}"),
        ("hat centre (Hz)", lambda d: f"{centroid(d['hats']):.0f}")]
for name, fn in rows:
    print(f"{name:26}{fn(orig):>12}{fn(halftime):>12}{fn(halfspeed):>12}")
hs_len = len(halfspeed['hats']) / fs
print(f"\nhalf-speed loop = {hs_len / (4 * beat):.2f} bars at {BPM} BPM, so it still lands on the original grid")
print(f"felt pulse (one snare per bar at {BPM} BPM) = {BPM/2:.0f} BPM for halftime and half speed")

print("\nHalfScrew CLUB HALFTIME strains (TIME, PITCH from halfscrew-fx.js v=44); export speed = TIME/100 x 2^(PITCH/12)")
print(f"   {'strain':18}{'TIME':>6}{'PITCH':>7}{'speed':>8}{'semitones':>11}{'140 BPM ->':>12}{'49 Hz 808 ->':>14}")
for name, tm, p in [("Half Pint Ackt", 50, 0), ("Full Pint Barr", 50, -2), ("Warehouse Roxx", 60, -1), ("Ackt Right", 65, 1), ("¾ Cup Qualli", 75, 0)]:
    r = tm / 100 * 2 ** (p / 12)
    print(f"   {name:18}{tm:5d}%{p:+7d}{r:8.3f}{12*np.log2(r):+11.2f}{140*r:9.1f} BPM{49*r:10.1f} Hz")

Reading the numbers

The snare is why they get confused. Snare to snare was 1.714 seconds in both versions, twice the 0.857 of the original. Your body hears one snare a bar either way, so both feel like 70. That’s the whole overlap.

The hats are where they split. In halftime, the hats still ticked every 0.107 seconds, real 16ths at 140, so the beat keeps its motion while the snare drags. At half speed they ticked every 0.214 seconds, 8ths at 140, and their centre dropped from 9161 to 4563 Hz. The top of the beat goes from shimmer to shuffle. If a halftime beat feels lazy instead of heavy, check the hats first.

The 808 changes jobs. Halftime left it at 49 Hz. Half speed put it at 24.5 Hz, with 95% of its energy below 30 Hz, and the part above 100 Hz, the harmonics a phone speaker actually plays, fell from 4.5% to 0.4%. The low end is still in the file. It just moved somewhere most speakers can’t follow, which is the phone speaker problem taken all the way.

The snare turns into a thud. Its body went from 200 Hz to 100 Hz. That’s a lot of the classic screwed weight, and it’s also why a half-speed snare can sit right on top of the kick.

Half speed still lands on the grid. The two bars became exactly 4.00 bars at 140 BPM. So a half-speed piece of audio drops into a 140 session without stretching. That’s useful, and it’s the trick in the list below.

Which one you want

  • A heavy drop in a trap beat? Halftime. Move the snare to 3, thin the kick, keep the hats moving and the 808 tuned. The song stays in key and on tempo, and any tempo-synced delays stay right because the project BPM never changed.
  • A screwed tape, an interlude, a slowed version of the whole record? Half speed, or something gentler. 50% is the extreme end. Most slowed versions live much higher, and this essay covers how to pick.
  • Both at once. Program halftime drums at the original tempo, then lay a half-speed copy of a melody, pad or vocal chop under them. Because half speed doubles the length exactly, a one-bar loop becomes two bars and stays locked to the grid, just an octave lower.
  • Halftime feel, original pitch, the whole bounce? That’s a time-stretch to 50%, not varispeed. It keeps the pitch and smears the transients. Three different jobs has the trade-offs.
  • A vocal at half speed drops an octave with its formants, so it reads as a character, not a singer. Great for a tag or an outro, hard work for a verse.

The club halftime strains, decoded

In HalfScrew, the CLUB HALFTIME family is half speed in the tape sense, plus a few in-between speeds. Half Pint Ackt is TIME 50 with no pitch change: exactly an octave, 140 BPM to 70. Full Pint Barr adds PITCH −2 on top, so the export speed is 0.445 and the drop is 14 semitones. A 49 Hz 808 lands at 21.8 Hz there. Warehouse Roxx, Ackt Right and ¾ Cup Qualli sit at 79.3, 96.4 and 105.0 BPM from a 140 beat, and the table in the output shows how far each drops in semitones. ¾ Cup Qualli comes out 2 cents from a whole semitone, while Ackt Right lands between keys, which is the out-of-key speed problem if anyone sings over it. None of these reprogram your drums. If you want halftime with the hats intact, that part happens in your session.

When someone asks for a halftime version, ask one question back: should it be lower? If yes, it’s half speed or a slowed version. If it should stay in key and keep the same BPM, it’s halftime, and it’s a drum edit.
Is halftime the same as half speed?
No. Halftime keeps the tempo and moves the snare to once a bar. Half speed plays the whole file at 50%, so it lasts twice as long and every sound drops an octave. In our test both put 1.714 s between snares at 140 BPM, but only half speed moved the 808 from 49 to 24.5 Hz.
What BPM is a halftime beat?
The project stays at its tempo, 140 stays 140, but with one snare a bar it feels like half of it, 70. That’s why tempo-synced delays and the grid still work in halftime.
Why does my 808 disappear when I play a beat at half speed?
Half speed halves every frequency. In our test a 49 Hz 808 fell to 24.5 Hz with 95% of its energy below 30 Hz, and the harmonics above 100 Hz that phone speakers play dropped from 4.5% to 0.4% of its energy.
How do I get a halftime feel without changing the pitch?
Reprogram the drums at the same tempo with the snare on 3, or time-stretch the bounce to 50%. Tape-style slowing always moves pitch with tempo.
