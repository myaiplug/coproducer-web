Sample rate and bit depth are delivery hygiene

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 02

CoProducer fails sample rates under 44.1 kHz. A 32 kHz MP3 labeled MASTER is not a master. Bit depth and rate mistakes are cheap to prevent and expensive to explain to a distributor.

The boring gate that saves recalls

Most storefronts expect at least 44.1 kHz. Working at 48 kHz is fine; delivering a bounced-down consumer encode is not. Check the file properties before you upload — or let the report fail you first in private.

Keep a bounce folder naming convention that includes rate and bit depth so assistants stop guessing.

Where it sits in the checklist

Sample rate sits beside LUFS, true peak, clips, phase, and score. It is not glamorous. It is why Friday night uploads get rejected Monday morning.

Measure once. Fix once. Ship.

Run the six-gate checklist in this essay before you open the distributor portal.
Is 96 kHz required?
No for most music delivery. Consistency and legality beat fetish sample rates.
Does CoProducer convert for you?
It measures and reports. Conversion is a separate bounce decision.
