Making a slowed outro? Switch to the slowed version on a bar line

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 06

To make a slowed outro, render a slowed version of the same bounce and switch to it on a bar line. The bar you switch on sits at its original time divided by the speed. In our test bar 25 of a 140 BPM beat was at 41.143 s in the original and 51.429 s in HalfScrew’s TIME 80 render. Cutting both files at 41.143 s landed 0.8 of a beat past bar 20’s downbeat instead.

The last chorus ends, and you want the record to sink: same beat, same vocal, suddenly slower and lower, like the tape got tired. You drop the slowed render on the track under the original, split both at the bar line, and the slowed side comes in somewhere in the middle of a phrase, a few bars back, half a beat off.

The idea is right. The cut point is wrong. A slowed render runs on a longer clock, so the same bar is later in that file.

Two renders, one bounce

Start from the exact bounce the original plays from, so the two files differ only in speed. In HalfScrew, set TIME, and PITCH if you want, and turn WOW to 0 for this one. WOW loads at 25 and nudges the timing as well as the pitch, which the wobble essay measured, and a switch needs both files on a straight clock. You can add wobble to the outro section afterwards.

If the slowed version is a ScrewAI strain, the file name tells you the flavor, not the speed. Divide the original’s length by the render’s to get it, as the release essay shows, and check the first kick after the switch by ear.

We wrote outro_switch.py to measure the switch. It builds a 32-bar beat at 140 BPM, slows it with HalfScrew’s resampler ported from the tool’s source, switches to the slowed render on the downbeat of bar 25 with different crossfades, and tries a tape-style ramp for comparison.

# A screwed outro: play the original, then switch to a slowed render of the same bounce on a bar line.
# Slowed render = HalfScrew's resampler ported from halfscrew.js resample() (v=44): 4-point Hermite (HQ), WOW 0.
# The ramp in part 4 is NOT a HalfScrew feature (its TIME is one fixed setting); it's what a DAW tempo ramp does.
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import butter, sosfilt
fs = 48000; rng = np.random.default_rng(1126); BPM = 140; beat = 60 / BPM; bar = 4 * beat

def hermite(y0, y1, y2, y3, f):
    c1 = 0.5 * (y2 - y0); c2 = y0 - 2.5 * y1 + 2 * y2 - 0.5 * y3; c3 = 0.5 * (y3 - y0) + 1.5 * (y1 - y2)
    return ((c3 * f + c2) * f + c1) * f + y1
def read_at(a, pos):
    i0 = np.floor(pos).astype(int); f = pos - i0
    g = lambda k: np.where((k >= 0) & (k < len(a)), a[np.clip(k, 0, len(a) - 1)], 0.0)
    return hermite(g(i0 - 1), g(i0), g(i0 + 1), g(i0 + 2), f)
def varispeed(a, ratio): return read_at(a, np.arange(int(np.ceil(len(a) / ratio))) * ratio)

# 32-bar beat at 140: kick, snare, hats, a sustained chord (so splices land mid-wave)
BARS = 32; n = int(round(BARS * bar * fs)); T = lambda s: np.arange(int(s * fs)) / fs
def place(hits, snd):
    y = np.zeros(n)
    for b in hits:
        i = int(round(b * beat * fs)); s = snd[: n - i]; y[i:i + len(s)] += s
    return y
kick = np.sin(2 * np.pi * np.cumsum(55 + 110 * np.exp(-T(.25) * 30)) / fs) * np.exp(-T(.25) * 12)
snare = (.5 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 190 * T(.2)) + sosfilt(butter(4, 1500, 'hp', fs=fs, output='sos'), rng.standard_normal(int(.2 * fs))) * .4) * np.exp(-T(.2) * 20)
hat = sosfilt(butter(4, [6000, 12000], 'bp', fs=fs, output='sos'), rng.standard_normal(int(.05 * fs))) * np.exp(-T(.05) * 90) * .3
kicks = [b * 4 + o for b in range(BARS) for o in (0, 2.5)]
chord = sum(.06 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * f * np.arange(n) / fs) for f in (220, 261.63, 329.63))
kick_trk = place(kicks, kick)
song = kick_trk + place([b * 4 + o for b in range(BARS) for o in (1, 3)], snare) + place([i / 2 for i in range(BARS * 8)], hat) + chord

def onsets(y, thr=.5):
    k = int(.003 * fs); env = np.sqrt(np.convolve(y ** 2, np.ones(k) / k, 'same'))
    above = env > thr * env.max(); idx = np.flatnonzero(above & ~np.r_[False, above[:-1]])
    keep = [idx[0]]
    for i in idx[1:]:
        if i - keep[-1] > .15 * fs: keep.append(i)
    return np.array(keep) / fs

SW = 24                                            # switch on the downbeat of bar 25 (start of the last 8 bars)
t0 = SW * bar
print(f"Song: {BARS} bars at {BPM} BPM = {n/fs:.2f} s. Switch to the slowed render at bar {SW+1}, {t0:.3f} s into the original.")

print("\n1) Where the switch lands in each slowed render (same bounce, WOW 0)")
print(f"   {'setting':28}{'speed':>8}{'semitones':>10}{'new BPM':>9}{'bar 25 in render':>18}{'last 8 bars':>13}{'song length':>13}")
for name, tm, p in (("TIME 80", 80, 0), ("POTION (TIME 100, PITCH -2)", 100, -2), ("TIME 70", 70, 0)):
    s = tm / 100 * 2 ** (p / 12)
    print(f"   {name:28}{s:8.4f}{12*np.log2(s):10.2f}{BPM*s:9.2f}{t0/s:16.3f} s{8*bar/s:11.2f} s{t0 + (n/fs - t0)/s:11.2f} s")
print(f"   original: last 8 bars {8*bar:.2f} s, song {n/fs:.2f} s")

s = .8; slow = varispeed(song, s); slow_k = varispeed(kick_trk, s); nb = beat / s
def switch(a, b, ta, tb, xf_ms):
    """a up to ta, then b from tb on; equal-power crossfade of xf_ms centred on the switch."""
    ia, ib = int(round(ta * fs)), int(round(tb * fs)); h = int(xf_ms / 1000 * fs / 2)
    if h == 0: return np.r_[a[:ia], b[ib:]], ia
    r = np.linspace(0, np.pi / 2, 2 * h); fo, fi = np.cos(r), np.sin(r)
    mid = a[ia - h:ia + h] * fo + b[ib - h:ib + h] * fi
    return np.r_[a[:ia - h], mid, b[ib + h:]], ia

print("\n2) Cutting the slowed render at the same timestamp vs at the same bar")
wrong = t0 / bar * s                               # bar position you land on in the slowed render at t0 seconds
print(f"   Same timestamp ({t0:.3f} s in both files): you land at bar {wrong + 1:.2f} of the slowed render, "
      f"{(wrong - np.floor(wrong)) * 4:.2f} beats past a downbeat ({(wrong - np.floor(wrong)) * bar / s * 1000:.1f} ms) and {SW - wrong:.1f} bars back in the song")
print(f"   Same bar: cut the slowed render at {t0:.3f} / {s} = {t0/s:.3f} s, on its own bar-25 downbeat")

print("\n3) The splice itself (switch on the bar, TIME 80): crossfade length vs hits that play out of step")
orig_hits = sorted(set([b * beat for b in kicks] + [(b * 4 + o) * beat for b in range(BARS) for o in (1, 3)] + [i / 2 * beat for i in range(BARS * 8)]))
slow_hits = [t / s for t in orig_hits]               # same hits, on the slowed render's clock, lined up at t0
print(f"   {'crossfade':>16}{'hits inside the fade, out of step':>36}")
for label, ms in (("none", 0), ("10 ms", 10), ("100 ms", 100), ("300 ms", 300), ("1 bar at 140", bar * 1000), ("2 bars at 140", 2 * bar * 1000)):
    h = ms / 2000; inside = lambda ts, off: [t - off for t in ts if abs(t - off) < h - .002 and abs(t - off) > .005]
    a_ = inside(orig_hits, t0); b_ = inside(slow_hits, t0 / s)
    print(f"   {label:>16}{len(a_):26d} old + {len(b_)} new")
print(f"   (drum hits sit on 8ths: {beat/2*1000:.1f} ms apart at 140, {nb/2*1000:.1f} ms at 112; both versions share the bar-25 downbeat)")
ko, _ = switch(kick_trk, slow_k, t0, t0 / s, 10); ks = onsets(ko, .3); ks = ks[ks > t0 - .01][:4]
print(f"   kicks after a 10 ms switch: {', '.join(f'{(k - t0)*1000:.1f}' for k in ks)} ms after the bar line "
      f"(112 BPM grid: 0, {2.5*nb*1000:.1f}, {4*nb*1000:.1f}, {6.5*nb*1000:.1f})")

print("\n4) A tape-style ramp instead of a switch (DAW tempo ramp, not a HalfScrew control): speed glides 1.0 -> 0.8")
for rb in (1, 2, 4):
    # speed falls linearly with source position over rb bars of the original, then holds at 0.8
    x0, L = t0 * fs, rb * bar * fs; pos = [0.0]; p = 0.0
    cut = int(round(t0 * fs)); seg_pos = []
    p = float(cut)
    while p < n:
        seg_pos.append(p); v = 1 - .2 * min(max((p - x0) / L, 0), 1); p += v
    seg_pos = np.array(seg_pos)
    ramp_k = np.r_[kick_trk[:cut], read_at(kick_trk, seg_pos)]
    ramp_dur = np.searchsorted(seg_pos, x0 + L) / fs
    ks = onsets(ramp_k, .3); after = ks[ks > t0 + ramp_dur - .01]
    print(f"   ramp over {rb} bar(s) of the original: plays for {ramp_dur:.3f} s (at 140: {rb*bar:.3f} s, at a straight 0.8: {rb*bar/.8:.3f} s); "
          f"first downbeat after it at {t0 + ramp_dur:.3f} s, kick spacing after it {np.diff(after[:3]).min()*1000:.1f}/{np.diff(after[:3]).max()*1000:.1f} ms")
print(f"   112 BPM grid after the ramp: beat {nb*1000:.1f} ms, kicks on 1 and the and-of-3 sit {2.5*nb*1000:.1f} / {1.5*nb*1000:.1f} ms apart")

Reading the numbers

The switch bar is the original time divided by the speed. Bar 25 is at 41.143 s in the original. At TIME 80 it’s at 51.429 s, at POTION (TIME 100, PITCH −2) at 46.181 s, at TIME 70 at 58.776 s. The last 8 bars stretch from 13.71 s to 17.14 s at TIME 80, so the whole song runs 58.29 s instead of 54.86.

Same timestamp is the classic mistake. Split both files at 41.143 s and you land at bar 20.20 of the slowed render: 0.80 of a beat, 428.6 ms, past a downbeat, and 4.8 bars back in the song. It sounds like a skip, because it is one.

Keep the crossfade short. The drums hit on 8ths, 214.3 ms apart at 140 and 267.9 ms at 112, and both versions share the bar-25 downbeat. Any fade up to 300 ms had no hits from either version playing out of step. A one-bar fade at 140 had 6 old and 6 new hits overlapping, two bars 14 and 12. A long fade also plays two keys at once, and TIME 80 is −3.86 semitones, between keys. After a 10 ms switch the kicks landed at −0.7, 1338.9, 2142.5 and 3481.8 ms past the bar line, right on the 112 BPM grid.

A ramp is a different move. HalfScrew’s TIME is one setting per export, so a glide comes from a tempo or varispeed ramp in your DAW. With the speed falling in a straight line from 1.0 to 0.8 over one bar of the original, that bar took 1.913 s, between the 1.714 s it takes at 140 and the 2.143 s at 112. After it, the kicks held the 112 BPM spacing, 803.6 and 1339.3 ms. The ramped audio is on its own clock, though, so keep playing from it rather than cutting back into the render.

The key matters if the outro has to sit next to something. POTION is exactly −2.00 semitones, a whole step down. TIME 80 isn’t on a semitone, which only matters if anything follows the outro or a long fade overlaps the two. Picking a speed on a semitone covers that.

A slowed outro in five moves

  1. Pick the switch bar and write down its time in the original. A section start works best: the last chorus, the bridge, the final 8.
  2. Divide by the speed to find the same bar in the slowed render. HalfScrew writes t and p into the export name, so t80-p0 is 0.8 and t100-p-2 is 0.8909.
  3. Split the slowed render there, line it up under the original’s bar line, and fade over 10 to 30 ms, centred on the downbeat.
  4. Change the session tempo at that bar, 140 to 112 here, decimals included for speeds like POTION’s 124.73, so anything you add after the switch lands on the new grid. Chopping a slowed beat has the grid math.
  5. For a screwed intro, run it backwards. Start in the slowed render and switch to the original at bar k, which sits at the original time of bar k divided by the speed in the slowed file.
Write two numbers on the session before you cut: the bar you switch on and the speed. The cut point in the slowed file is one division.
How do I make a slowed outro?
Render a slowed version of the same bounce, then switch to it on a bar line with a short crossfade. In the slowed file, that bar sits at its original time divided by the speed.
Where do I cut the slowed version?
At the original time divided by the speed. Bar 25 of a 140 BPM beat is at 41.143 s, so in a TIME 80 render it’s at 51.429 s and in a POTION render (TIME 100, PITCH −2) at 46.181 s.
How long should the crossfade be?
Short. In our test any fade up to 300 ms had no drum hits playing out of step. A one-bar fade had 6 from each version overlapping.
Can I make a screwed intro the same way?
Yes. Start in the slowed render and switch to the original on a bar line. The bar is at its original time divided by the speed in the slowed file.
