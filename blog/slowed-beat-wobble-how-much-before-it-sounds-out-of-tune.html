Tape wobble on a slowed beat: how much before it sounds out of tune?

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 06

Tape wobble pulls the pitch of a slowed beat up and down around the note, so the question isn’t whether it’s out of tune but how far and how often. In HalfScrew’s WOW, measured on a rendered tone, the default 25% swung the pitch ±11.2 cents and spent 31% of the time more than 10 cents off. 50% swung ±22.5 cents, and 100% swung ±45 cents, nearly half a semitone. On its own that’s character. Under a tuned vocal or a fresh keys part, it’s the part that drifts.

You slow the beat, add a little tape wobble, and it finally sounds like an old cassette left in a hot car. Perfect. Then the artist records over it with Auto-Tune on, or you add a clean piano, and every few seconds the two of them seem to disagree. The beat is fine. The new part is fine. They just aren’t sitting on the same pitch at the same time.

What wobble actually does

Wow is a slow change in playback speed, the way a worn tape or a warped record speeds up and slows down. Since it’s speed, it moves pitch and timing together: the note bends sharp, then flat, and the groove leans a little early, then a little late. We ported HalfScrew’s resampler from its source into wow_check.py, rendered an A3 tone slowed to 80% at each WOW setting, and tracked the pitch sample by sample against the steady slowed note. Then we listed the strains that use WOW, with how far their speed already sits from a whole semitone.

# Tape wobble on a slowed beat: how far HalfScrew's WOW knob moves pitch and timing, measured on a rendered tone.
# The resampler is ported from halfscrew.js resample() (v=44): wobble = sin(2*pi*0.35*t) * (WOW/100) * 0.45 semitones,
# applied to the read speed, 4-point Hermite interpolation (HQ on).
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import hilbert
fs = 48000

def hermite(y0, y1, y2, y3, f):
    c1 = 0.5 * (y2 - y0); c2 = y0 - 2.5 * y1 + 2 * y2 - 0.5 * y3; c3 = 0.5 * (y3 - y0) + 1.5 * (y1 - y2)
    return ((c3 * f + c2) * f + c1) * f + y1

def halfscrew_resample(a, ratio, wow):            # wow = knob / 100
    n_out = int(np.ceil(len(a) / ratio)) - 4; i = np.arange(n_out)
    wobble = np.sin(i / fs * 2 * np.pi * 0.35) * wow * 0.45            # semitones
    step = ratio * 2 ** (wobble / 12)
    pos = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(step)[:-1]])
    keep = pos < len(a) - 3; pos = pos[keep]; i0 = np.floor(pos).astype(int); f = pos - i0
    g = lambda k: a[np.clip(k, 0, len(a) - 1)]
    return hermite(g(i0 - 1), g(i0), g(i0 + 1), g(i0 + 2), f), pos

def pitch_track(y):                               # instantaneous frequency from the analytic signal
    ph = np.unwrap(np.angle(hilbert(y))); fi = np.diff(ph) * fs / (2 * np.pi)
    k = int(0.02 * fs); fi = np.convolve(fi, np.ones(k) / k, 'valid'); return fi[fs // 2: -fs // 2]

tone = np.sin(2 * np.pi * 220 * np.arange(int(fs * 14)) / fs)   # A3, 14 s of source
ratio = 0.80
flat, pos0 = halfscrew_resample(tone, ratio, 0.0)
f_ref = np.median(pitch_track(flat))
print(f"A3 (220 Hz) slowed to {ratio:.0%} with HalfScrew's resampler: steady note {f_ref:.1f} Hz")
print(f"WOW rate 0.35 Hz: one full wobble every {1/0.35:.2f} s = {112/60/0.35:.2f} beats at 112 BPM, {90/60/0.35:.2f} beats at 90 BPM\n")
print(f"   {'WOW':>5}{'peak drift':>13}{'time >5 c off':>16}{'time >10 c off':>17}{'beat vs in-tune part':>23}{'groove drift':>15}")
for w in (0, 10, 25, 35, 50, 80, 100):
    y, pos = halfscrew_resample(tone, ratio, w / 100)
    c = 1200 * np.log2(pitch_track(y) / f_ref)
    n = min(len(pos), len(pos0)); drift_ms = np.max(np.abs(pos[:n] - pos0[:n])) / ratio / fs * 1000
    beat = f_ref * (2 ** (np.max(np.abs(c)) / 1200) - 1)
    print(f"   {w:4d}%{np.max(np.abs(c)):9.1f} cents{100*np.mean(np.abs(c) > 5):13.0f}%{100*np.mean(np.abs(c) > 10):14.0f}%{beat:17.1f} Hz max{drift_ms:11.1f} ms")

print("\nHalfScrew strains and presets that use WOW (TIME, PITCH, WOW from halfscrew-fx.js / halfscrew.js v=44)")
print(f"   {'strain':22}{'TIME':>6}{'PITCH':>7}{'WOW':>5}{'wobble peak':>14}{'speed off nearest semitone':>29}{'worst case':>13}")
S = [("Tussy Pour", 78, 0, 10), ("Hi-Tekk Purp Dreamz", 88, 0, 20), ("Midnight Wockk", 92, 0, 6), ("Dusty Qualli", 94, 0, 15),
     ("Roxx Grape Rain", 90, 0, 12), ("Basement Barr", 96, 0, 20), ("Triss Light", 92, 1, 5), ("3AM Triss", 97, 0, 10),
     ("Wobbly Wockk", 96, 0, 35), ("Sea-Sick Hi-Tekk", 94, 0, 80), ("Old Roxx", 93, 0, 50), ("Tuss Lite", 98, 0, 20),
     ("Akorne Melt", 88, 0, 100), ("POTION (preset)", 100, -2, 40), ("DEEP (preset)", 70, -4, 60), ("default knob", 100, 0, 25)]
for name, t, p, w in S:
    semis = 12 * np.log2(t / 100) + p; off = 100 * (semis - round(semis)); wob = 45 * w / 100
    print(f"   {name:22}{t:5d}%{p:+6d}{w:5d}{wob:10.1f} c{off:+24.1f} c{abs(off) + wob:11.1f} c")

Reading the numbers

Each 10% of WOW is about 4.5 cents. The wobble peaks at 0.45 semitones times the knob, so 25% is ±11.2 cents, 50% is ±22.5 and 100% is ±45. The pitch we tracked on the rendered tone matched that formula.

It’s not just the peaks. At 25% the pitch was more than 10 cents off 31% of the time. At 50% that rose to 72%, and at 80% to 83%. A wobble spends most of its cycle away from the note, so a higher setting isn’t an occasional bend, it’s most of the song.

Against an in-tune part, you hear it as slow beating. Our A3 slowed to 176 Hz. At WOW 25% it drifted up to 1.1 Hz away from an in-tune copy at the peaks; at 100%, 4.6 Hz. That’s a slow throb that comes and goes with the wobble, once every 2.86 seconds.

The groove moves too. Speed changes shift time, so the slowed beat drifted up to 6.1 ms off its straight grid at 25%, 12.5 ms at 50% and 26.3 ms at 100%. You won’t notice it on the beat alone. You can notice it when you lay new, quantized drums on top of a heavily wobbled bounce.

It isn’t synced to the song. One full wobble every 2.86 seconds is 5.33 beats at 112 BPM and 4.29 at 90. It drifts across the bar instead of landing in the same place every loop, which is part of why it sounds like tape and not like an effect.

When wobble stacks on the speed

The strain table is the part worth a second look. A speed that isn’t a whole number of semitones already leaves the beat between keys (that’s this essay), and wobble swings on top of that offset. Dusty Qualli sits 7.1 cents off a semitone with ±6.8 cents of wobble, 13.9 at worst. Akorne Melt sits 21.3 cents off with WOW at 100, so at the peak it’s 66.3 cents away from the nearest note. The POTION preset gets around it: PITCH −2 lands exactly on a semitone, so its 18.0 cents of wobble swing around a real note.

How much to use

  • Just releasing a slowed version? Use as much as sounds good. Nobody is tuning against it, and the drift is the vibe.
  • Someone will sing or play over it? Give them a bounce with WOW at 0 to write and record on, and add the wobble to the final. Hard-tuned vocals are the least forgiving, since Auto-Tune holds the voice steady while the beat moves.
  • Want wobble and a tuned part together? Pick a speed on a semitone first, then keep WOW low. WOW 10% swung only ±4.5 cents around the note in our render.
  • Layering new drums? Keep heavy wobble off the stem you’re drumming to, or put the wobble on after the drums are in, so both move together.
  • Using it as a moment? A heavy setting on an intro, a breakdown or an outro reads as a choice. All song long, it reads as a warped tape.

HalfScrew opens with WOW at 25%, so if you load a track, slow it and export without touching that knob, you already have ±11.2 cents of wobble baked in. Turn WOW to 0 for the writing bounce. Its PITCH knob moves in whole semitones, so TIME at 100% with PITCH set where you want lands on a real note, and the wobble swings around that. The export filename carries the w value next to t and p, so the bounce you hand off tells you how much wobble is in it. The TAPE WARBLE strains are the heavy end, from Tuss Lite at 20 up to Akorne Melt at 100.

Wobble and vibrato on a vocal are different jobs. A singer’s vibrato swings around the note on purpose and on their own breath. Tape wobble moves the whole beat under them. When the two fight, the fix is usually less wobble on the beat, not more tuning on the voice. And if it’s the retune itself that sounds robotic, that’s a retune speed job.
Does tape wobble make a beat out of tune?
It moves it in and out of tune. In our render HalfScrew’s default WOW 25% swung the pitch ±11.2 cents and was more than 10 cents off 31% of the time. At 100% it swung ±45 cents.
How much wow is too much on a slowed beat?
On its own, whatever sounds good. Under a tuned vocal or a new instrument, keep it low: WOW 10% stayed within ±4.5 cents, while 50% spent 72% of the time more than 10 cents off.
Does wow and flutter change the timing?
Yes, a little, because it changes speed. The slowed beat drifted up to 6.1 ms off its grid at WOW 25% and 26.3 ms at 100%.
Should I record vocals over a beat with tape wobble?
Record over a version with the wobble off, then put the wobble on the final mix. That keeps a tuned vocal from fighting a beat that keeps moving.
