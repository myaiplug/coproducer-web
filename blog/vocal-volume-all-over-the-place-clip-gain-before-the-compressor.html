Vocal volume all over the place? Clip-gain the phrases before the compressor

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 06

If a vocal is quiet in some lines and loud in others, even out the phrases with clip gain or volume automation first, then let the compressor handle the small stuff. A compressor only reacts to what crosses its threshold, so it squashes the loud lines and ignores the quiet ones. In our test a take with a 21 dB gap between its loudest and quietest phrase still had an 8.6 dB gap after a 4:1 compressor. With clip-gain rides first, the same compressor left a 0.1 dB gap and worked the same 5 dB on every line.

You sing the verse close to the mic, back off on the second line, belt the hook, then mumble an ad-lib at the end. Totally normal take. Then you slap a compressor on it, turn the threshold down until the hook stops jumping out, and the verse still disappears under the beat. So you push the compressor harder, and now the hook sounds flattened and the breaths come up.

That’s not a bad compressor. It’s the wrong tool for that job.

Why the compressor can’t fix it alone

A compressor turns down whatever goes over its threshold, by an amount set by its ratio. Anything under the threshold passes through untouched. So if one line is way louder than another, the loud line gets most of the work and the quiet line gets none, and the gap only shrinks by part of the difference. We tested it in vocal_ride_check.py: four sung-style phrases at very different levels, each word wobbling ±3 dB, through the same plain compressor with and without clip gain first.

# One compressor on an uneven vocal take, with and without clip-gain rides first. Synthetic "vocal", fixed seed.
import numpy as np
fs = 48000; rng = np.random.default_rng(17)
db = lambda x: 20 * np.log10(max(x, 1e-12))
rms = lambda x: np.sqrt(np.mean(x ** 2))

def phrase(f0, dur, level_db):
    """A sung-ish line: harmonic tone with vibrato, eight syllables (each +/-3 dB), breathy noise."""
    t = np.arange(int(fs * dur)) / fs
    f = f0 * (1 + 0.006 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 5.5 * t))
    ph = 2 * np.pi * np.cumsum(f) / fs
    x = sum(np.sin(k * ph) / k for k in range(1, 12)) + 0.02 * rng.standard_normal(t.size)
    syl = np.clip(np.sin(np.pi * (t * 8 / dur) % np.pi), 0, 1) ** 0.6   # eight swells
    word = 10 ** (rng.uniform(-3, 3, 8) / 20)                             # words vary +/-3 dB
    x = x * syl * word[np.minimum((t * 8 / dur).astype(int), 7)]
    return x / rms(x) * 10 ** (level_db / 20)

# Four phrases as recorded: steady, a quiet tail-off, one belted line, a mumbled ad-lib
take = [("verse line 1", 220, -20), ("verse line 2 (backs off the mic)", 196, -29),
        ("hook line (belted)", 262, -11), ("ad-lib (mumbled)", 175, -32)]
gap = np.zeros(int(fs * 0.25))
parts = [phrase(f, 2.0, lv) for _, f, lv in take]

def compress(x, thr=-24.0, ratio=4.0, att=0.005, rel=0.08):
    """Feed-forward RMS-ish compressor, hard knee. Returns output and gain reduction (dB, per sample)."""
    a, r = np.exp(-1 / (att * fs)), np.exp(-1 / (rel * fs))
    env = 0.0; gr = np.empty(x.size); lvl = np.abs(x)
    for i, v in enumerate(lvl):
        env = a * env + (1 - a) * v if v > env else r * env + (1 - r) * v
        e = db(env); over = max(e - thr, 0.0)
        gr[i] = over - over / ratio
    return x * 10 ** (-gr / 20), gr

def report(name, segs):
    x = np.concatenate([np.concatenate([s, gap]) for s in segs])
    y, gr = compress(x)
    if name: print(name)
    i = 0; lv = []
    for (label, _, _), s in zip(take, segs):
        n = s.size; yo = y[i:i + n]; g = gr[i:i + n]
        lv.append(db(rms(yo)))
        print(f"   {label:34s} in {db(rms(s)):6.1f} dBFS -> out {lv[-1]:6.1f} dBFS | gain reduction avg {g.mean():4.1f} dB, max {g.max():4.1f} dB")
        i += n + gap.size
    print(f"   loudest minus quietest phrase after the compressor: {max(lv) - min(lv):.1f} dB")
    return lv

print("Compressor: threshold -24 dBFS, 4:1, 5 ms attack, 80 ms release (same settings both runs)\n")
print("1) Raw take straight into the compressor")
report("", parts)
print("\n2) Clip gain first: each phrase turned to -20 dBFS RMS, then the same compressor")
rides = [-20 - lv for _, _, lv in take]
print("   clip-gain moves: " + ", ".join(f"{r:+.0f} dB" for r in rides))
report("", [p * 10 ** (r / 20) for p, r in zip(parts, rides)])

print("\n3) Raw take, compressor pushed to catch the quiet lines (threshold -34 dBFS, 8:1)")
x = np.concatenate([np.concatenate([s, gap]) for s in parts])
y, gr = compress(x, thr=-34.0, ratio=8.0)
i = 0; lv = []
for (label, _, _), s in zip(take, parts):
    n = s.size; lv.append(db(rms(y[i:i + n]))); g = gr[i:i + n]
    print(f"   {label:34s} out {lv[-1]:6.1f} dBFS | gain reduction avg {g.mean():4.1f} dB, max {g.max():4.1f} dB")
    i += n + gap.size
print(f"   loudest minus quietest phrase: {max(lv) - min(lv):.1f} dB")

Reading the numbers

Straight in, the compressor only fixed the loud lines. The take went from −11 dBFS on the belted hook to −32 dBFS on the mumbled ad-lib, a 21 dB spread. After the compressor the hook was down to −23.4 dBFS, with 11.6 dB of gain reduction on average and peaks of 15.0 dB. The second verse line got 0.1 dB of compression and the ad-lib got none at all. The gap between loudest and quietest phrase was still 8.6 dB. That’s the verse that keeps sinking under the beat.

Pushing harder evens it out by crushing the hook. Dropping the threshold to −34 dBFS at 8:1 got the spread down to 2.7 dB, but the hook was now taking 22.3 dB of gain reduction on average, 26.3 dB at the peaks, and even the first verse line was being squeezed 14.4 dB. That’s where vocals start sounding flat, breathy and pumpy, and every consonant and breath between words comes up with them.

Clip gain first, and the compressor gets an easy job. We turned each phrase to the same level before the compressor: +9 dB on the line that backed off the mic, −9 dB on the hook, +12 dB on the ad-lib. Same compressor, same settings. Every line came out at −25.6 or −25.7 dBFS with about 5 dB of average gain reduction, and the gap between phrases was 0.1 dB. The words inside each line still moved around, which is exactly what you want the compressor smoothing.

Real takes won’t come out that tidy, since we matched each phrase exactly and by ear you’ll get within a dB or two. That’s still the difference between a compressor working evenly and one that’s either asleep or crushing.

How to ride a vocal before compressing

  1. Loop the vocal with the beat and listen line by line. Mark the lines that dip under the beat and the ones that jump out.
  2. Split the clip at the start of each problem line and use clip gain (or region gain, or pre-fader volume automation) to bring it in line. Big moves are fine; in our take they were up to 12 dB.
  3. Do words next, only where one word still pokes out or drops away. Small moves, a couple of dB.
  4. Now set the compressor. Aim for a few dB of gain reduction on most lines, not 15 on some and none on others. If one line still needs much more than the rest, go back and ride it.
  5. Do your clip gain before the compressor in the chain. Fader automation after the compressor changes the level you hear, not what the compressor reacts to.

A quick way to check you got it right: bounce the vocal with and without the rides, put both against the beat, and flip between them in CoProducer’s A/B. Listen for the verse lines. If they sit where the hook sits without the hook sounding squashed, you’re done.

Ride the de-esser too. If a phrase got turned up 9 dB with clip gain, its esses came up 9 dB as well, so set the de-esser after the rides, not before.
Should I use clip gain or compression to even out vocals?
Both, in that order. Clip gain fixes big phrase-to-phrase jumps, then the compressor smooths words. In our test the compressor alone left an 8.6 dB gap between phrases; with clip gain first it was 0.1 dB.
Why is my vocal still quiet in the verse after compression?
The quiet lines probably never reach the threshold. In our test the softest lines got 0.1 dB and 0 dB of gain reduction while the belted hook got 11.6 dB.
How much gain reduction should a vocal compressor do?
A few dB on most lines is a good place to start. Our ridden take needed about 5 dB on average everywhere. If one section needs 20 dB or more, ride it with clip gain first.
Does clip gain go before or after the compressor?
Before. Clip gain changes what the compressor hears. Fader automation after the compressor only changes the final level.
