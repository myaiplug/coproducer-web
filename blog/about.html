About Lacquer and NoDAW Labs

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer · Updated 19 October 2026

Lacquer is the journal of CoProducer, written and published by NoDAW Labs. NoDAW Labs makes local-first audio tools for producers and mix engineers: CoProducer for measuring and repairing masters, reTUNE[432] for 432 Hz retuning, and DeepSplit for stem separation. The essays are practical notes on loudness, true peak, stems and delivery.

What Lacquer is

Lacquer is a set of answer-first essays for people who bounce masters. Each piece opens with a short answer you can quote, then gives the session advice underneath: what to measure, in what order, and when to stop. Topics include integrated and short-term LUFS, true peak and codecs, clipping, phase and mono, stems, 432 Hz retuning and release checklists.

The tools

CoProducer

CoProducer is local-first audio analysis and conservative repair, not a cloud mastering service. It measures integrated loudness against a −14 LUFS streaming target (pass within 1.5 LU), true peak against a −1.0 dBTP ceiling, clipped samples, sample rate and phase correlation, and gives a release score where 90 out of 100 counts as release-ready. Auto-repair may apply loudness normalization, a true-peak limiter and a gentle 25 Hz high-pass. It does not rebuild clipped transients and it does not fix phase for you. The website Analyze page shows projected post-repair numbers; the Windows desktop app writes the repaired WAV. There is a free demo, and Pro is $49 one-time, not a subscription (pricing).

reTUNE[432]

reTUNE[432] retunes audio you own from 440 Hz to 432 Hz. Its browser engine makes a free local preview of a track without uploading it, and a synced A/B player compares the two versions. The full reTUNE[432] system for Windows is an offline FFmpeg batch engine with alternate reference pitches and optional LUFS targets. The 432 Hz series on Lacquer covers the math, the history and the release checks behind it.

DeepSplit

DeepSplit separates songs into stems on your own computer. The desktop app runs Demucs-, MDX- and Roformer-class separation models locally, on a GPU if you have one, and is built to work offline once installed. It goes from two stems up to drum sub-separation and writes lossless stems.

The CoProducer site also hosts StemSplit and ScrewAI demos; you can try both from the CoProducer home page.
