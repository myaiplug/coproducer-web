Screwed vocals hard to understand? EQ after you slow, not before

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 06

A screwed vocal gets hard to understand because slowing moves every frequency down by the same ratio as the speed. At 80%, bite that sat at 5 kHz lands at 4 kHz, and low end that sat above the body slides into it. In our test, slowing a vocal to 80% put 5.6 dB more of its energy into 150 to 500 Hz and took 3.0 dB out of 8 to 12 kHz. So EQ after you slow, not before: a 3 kHz presence boost made before slowing ended up at 2.4 kHz.

The beat slows down beautifully. Everything gets heavy and syrupy, the 808 turns into a slab, and then the verse comes in and you can’t make out half the words. The voice is there. It’s big and dark and it sounds great on the hook. It just stopped talking clearly.

That’s not a mix mistake. Every part of the voice moved down, the part that makes words included. So the fix lives after the slowdown, at the new frequencies, not at the old ones.

What slowing does to a voice

Tape-style slowing (varispeed) plays the samples back slower, so every frequency in the vocal drops by the speed: at 80%, 3 kHz becomes 2.4 kHz and 5 kHz becomes 4 kHz. The formants go down too, which is why a screwed singer sounds like a giant. The formants essay covers that side. This one is about the words.

We checked it in vocal_eq_check.py: a synthetic sung line around 180 Hz with four formants and short consonant bursts, slowed to 90, 85 and 80% with 4-point Hermite interpolation (what HalfScrew’s HQ mode uses). Then we measured how much of the vocal’s energy sat in each band, and tried the same presence boost before and after slowing.

# Screwed vocal clarity: where the "words" band goes when a vocal is slowed tape-style,
# and whether a presence boost works better before or after slowing. Synthetic vowel + consonants, fixed seed.
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import lfilter, butter, sosfilt
fs = 48000; rng = np.random.default_rng(11)

def vowel(dur=4.0, formants=((700, 90), (1200, 110), (2600, 160), (3400, 200))):
    t = np.arange(int(fs * dur)) / fs; y = np.zeros_like(t)
    f0 = 180 * 2 ** ((3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 0.5 * t) + 0.3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 5.5 * t)) / 12)   # sung line: +-3 semitones + vibrato
    ph = 2 * np.pi * np.cumsum(f0) / fs
    for k in range(1, 60):
        f = k * f0
        g = sum(1 / (1 + ((f - F) / B) ** 2) for F, B in formants) + 0.02     # formant envelope follows the moving harmonic
        y += np.where(f < 12000, g / k ** 0.5, 0) * np.sin(k * ph + rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi))
    return y / np.max(np.abs(y))

def consonants(dur=4.0):                          # "s" / "t" bursts, 3-12 kHz noise, 6 per second
    n = int(fs * dur); y = np.zeros(n); sos = butter(4, [3000, 12000], 'bandpass', fs=fs, output='sos')
    for s in np.arange(0.05, dur - 0.1, 1 / 6):
        L = int(fs * 0.06); i = int(s * fs); y[i:i + L] += rng.standard_normal(L) * np.hanning(L)
    return sosfilt(sos, y)

def peaking(y, f, gain_db, q=1.0):                # RBJ cookbook bell (same family HalfScrew's 8-band EQ uses)
    A = 10 ** (gain_db / 40); w = 2 * np.pi * f / fs; al = np.sin(w) / (2 * q)
    b = [1 + al * A, -2 * np.cos(w), 1 - al * A]; a = [1 + al / A, -2 * np.cos(w), 1 - al / A]
    return lfilter(np.array(b) / a[0], np.array(a) / a[0], y)

def slow(y, speed):                               # tape-style: time and pitch together, 4-point Hermite (HalfScrew HQ)
    pos = np.arange(1, len(y) - 3, speed); i = pos.astype(int); f = pos - i
    y0, y1, y2, y3 = y[i - 1], y[i], y[i + 1], y[i + 2]
    c1 = 0.5 * (y2 - y0); c2 = y0 - 2.5 * y1 + 2 * y2 - 0.5 * y3; c3 = 0.5 * (y3 - y0) + 1.5 * (y1 - y2)
    return ((c3 * f + c2) * f + c1) * f + y1

def spectrum(y):
    Y = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(y * np.hanning(len(y)))) ** 2; fr = np.fft.rfftfreq(len(y), 1 / fs); return fr, Y

BANDS = [("body 150-500 Hz", 150, 500), ("words 2-4 kHz", 2000, 4000), ("bite 4-8 kHz", 4000, 8000), ("air 8-12 kHz", 8000, 12000)]
def band_db(y):                                   # share of total energy in each band, dB
    fr, Y = spectrum(y); tot = Y[(fr > 60) & (fr < 16000)].sum()
    return [10 * np.log10(Y[(fr >= lo) & (fr < hi)].sum() / tot) for _, lo, hi in BANDS]

def centroid(y, lo=2000, hi=12000):                # 'top-end centre': energy-weighted mean frequency, 2-12 kHz
    fr, Y = spectrum(y); m = (fr >= lo) & (fr < hi); return (fr[m] * Y[m]).sum() / Y[m].sum()

v = vowel() + 0.35 * consonants(); v /= np.max(np.abs(v))
print("Synthetic vocal: sung line around 180 Hz, formants at 700 / 1200 / 2600 / 3400 Hz, consonant bursts 3-12 kHz\n")
print("1) Where the energy sits (share of the vocal's total energy, dB; closer to 0 = more of it)")
print(f"   {'':16}" + "".join(f"{b[0]:>21}" for b in BANDS) + f"{'top-end centre':>19}")
rows = {}
for name, sp in (("original", 1.0), ("slowed to 90%", 0.90), ("slowed to 85%", 0.85), ("slowed to 80%", 0.80)):
    y = v if sp == 1 else slow(v, sp); rows[sp] = band_db(y)
    print(f"   {name:16}" + "".join(f"{x:19.1f} dB" for x in rows[sp]) + f"{centroid(y):15.0f} Hz")
d = [b - a for a, b in zip(rows[1.0], rows[0.80])]
print("   change at 80%  " + "".join(f"{x:+19.1f} dB" for x in d))
print("   formant at 2600 Hz lands at %.0f Hz at 80%%; 3400 Hz lands at %.0f Hz" % (2600 * 0.8, 3400 * 0.8))

print("\n2) Same +4 dB bell at 3 kHz (Q 1.0), applied before slowing vs after slowing to 80%")
base = slow(v, 0.8); fr, Yb = spectrum(base)
def boost_centre(y):                              # where the boost actually ended up: peak of (EQ'd / plain) spectrum
    _, Y = spectrum(y); r = 10 * np.log10(np.convolve(Y, np.ones(400), 'same') / np.convolve(Yb, np.ones(400), 'same'))
    m = (fr > 500) & (fr < 10000); return fr[m][np.argmax(r[m])], r[m].max()
pre = slow(peaking(v, 3000, 4), 0.8); post = peaking(base, 3000, 4)
for name, y in (("EQ before slowing", pre), ("EQ after slowing", post)):
    c, g = boost_centre(y); b = band_db(y)
    print(f"   {name:18} boost centred at {c:5.0f} Hz (+{g:.1f} dB)   bite 4-8 kHz {b[2]:6.1f} dB   top-end centre {centroid(y):5.0f} Hz")
print(f"   (plain 80% slow:   bite 4-8 kHz {rows[0.80][2]:6.1f} dB   top-end centre {centroid(base):5.0f} Hz; original bite {rows[1.0][2]:.1f} dB, centre {centroid(v):.0f} Hz)")
shelf = peaking(base, 5000, 4, 0.7)
print(f"   +4 dB at 5 kHz (Q 0.7) after slowing: bite 4-8 kHz {band_db(shelf)[2]:6.1f} dB   top-end centre {centroid(shelf):5.0f} Hz")

print("\n3) Where a frequency in the original ends up after slowing")
print(f"   {'original':>10}" + "".join(f"{s:>9.0%}" for s in (0.9, 0.85, 0.8, 0.75)))
for f in (300, 1000, 3000, 5000, 8000):
    print(f"   {f:8} Hz" + "".join(f"{f * s:9.0f}" for s in (0.9, 0.85, 0.8, 0.75)))

print("\n4) A clarity move after slowing to 80%: -3 dB at 300 Hz (Q 1.0) and +3 dB at 5 kHz (Q 0.7), vs the original")
fix = peaking(peaking(base, 300, -3, 1.0), 5000, 3, 0.7)
print(f"   {'':22}" + "".join(f"{b[0]:>21}" for b in BANDS) + f"{'top-end centre':>19}")
for name, y in (("original", v), ("slowed to 80%", base), ("slowed 80% + fix", fix)):
    print(f"   {name:22}" + "".join(f"{x:19.1f} dB" for x in band_db(y)) + f"{centroid(y):15.0f} Hz")

Reading the numbers

The body piles up. At 80%, the 150 to 500 Hz band went from −16.6 to −11.0 dB of the vocal’s total, 5.6 dB more. Harmonics that used to sit above the body moved down into it. At 90% it was only 0.8 dB more, at 85% 2.1 dB. That’s the boxy, chesty part of a screwed vocal, and it’s also where the 808’s upper harmonics and the low-mid of the beat live.

The air drops out. 8 to 12 kHz lost 3.0 dB at 80%, and the energy-weighted centre of everything above 2 kHz fell from 3549 to 2967 Hz. The 2 to 4 kHz band barely changed in level, −4.6 to −5.0 dB, but what’s in it changed: the old 2.5 to 5 kHz content now sits there, and the part that used to be in it moved lower. The words aren’t gone. They’re just sitting on top of a thicker body, with less edge on the consonants.

EQ before slowing moves with the voice. A +4 dB bell at 3 kHz put on before slowing ended up centred at 2399 Hz. Put on after, it stayed at 3008 Hz. If you EQ the bounce and then slow it, every move you made slides down by the speed. A presence boost turns into a honk, and a de-mud cut at 300 Hz ends up at 240.

A small move after slowing gets most of it back. −3 dB at 300 Hz and +3 dB at 5 kHz, both after the slowdown, brought the body back down to −13.0 dB and the bite up from −15.1 to −12.7 dB, with the top-end centre back up to 3081 Hz. It isn’t the original voice, and it shouldn’t be. It’s the screwed voice, with the words back in front.

How to keep the words

  1. Slow first, then EQ, so you’re hearing the voice you’ll release. If you have to EQ the vocal before slowing, multiply the frequency by the speed to see where it’ll land.
  2. Listen to the verse, not the hook. Hooks are usually repeated and known, so they read even when they’re dark. Verses are where words get lost.
  3. Take a little body out first. A gentle cut in the 200 to 400 Hz range on the slowed vocal or the slowed mix often does more than any boost. The low-mid pileup essay has the way to find the exact spot.
  4. Then add bite at the new frequencies, around 4 to 6 kHz, a few dB and broad. Small moves. The darkness is the point of the sound.
  5. If the vocal is fighting the beat in 2 to 4 kHz, cut the beat there instead of pushing the voice. Vocal buried in the beat shows why.
  6. If it’s a stems session, you can slow the vocal and the beat separately and EQ only the vocal. On a stereo bounce, the move hits the whole mix, so go gentler.

In HalfScrew the 8-band EQ already sits after the slowdown in the chain, so its bands work on the slowed voice. Bell 3 starts at 3200 Hz and Bell 4 at 7500 Hz, and the Vocal Presence EQ preset is a sensible place to start: it trims 220 Hz and lifts around 3 and 5.5 kHz. Some of the strains darken the top on purpose. The CLASSIC HOUSTON ones pull the 2.5 kHz high shelf down 2 to 4 dB, so if the words vanish on one of those, that shelf is the first thing to check.

If you’re still deciding how slow, ScrewAI renders several strains from one file in a single batch (17 flavors in the drag-and-drop .bat version, 60+ in the app). Line up two or three speeds of the same verse and listen for the one where the words still read. That’s usually a better first move than EQ-ing the slowest one until it talks.

Don’t chase the original. A screwed vocal is supposed to be darker and heavier than the record. The goal is that you can follow the words on a phone speaker, not that it sounds like it was never slowed.
Why do slowed vocals sound muffled?
Slowing moves every frequency down by the speed. At 80% the 5 kHz bite lands at 4 kHz and harmonics slide into the body. In our test the 150 to 500 Hz band rose 5.6 dB and 8 to 12 kHz dropped 3.0 dB.
Should I EQ a vocal before or after slowing it?
After. EQ made before slowing moves down with the voice: a 3 kHz boost before slowing to 80% ended up centred at 2399 Hz in our test. After slowing it stayed at 3008 Hz.
How do I make a screwed vocal clearer?
Slow first, then take a little out around 200 to 400 Hz and add a few dB of broad bite around 4 to 6 kHz. In our test −3 dB at 300 Hz and +3 dB at 5 kHz after slowing pulled the body back 2 dB and lifted the bite 2.4 dB.
Where does 3 kHz end up after slowing a song?
At the original frequency times the speed: 2700 Hz at 90%, 2550 Hz at 85%, 2400 Hz at 80% and 2250 Hz at 75%.
