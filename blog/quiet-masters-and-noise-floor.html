Quiet masters get turned up — and so does the noise

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 02

Streaming turn-up applies to quiet files as well as turn-down to hot ones. A −20 LUFS master may be gained up, which lifts hiss, rumble, and room tone with it. Measure noise floor before you celebrate “dynamic.”

Both directions of normalization

Engineers obsess over hot masters getting turned down. The other side matters: sparse, quiet prints get lifted. If your noise floor sits near −40 dBFS in a sparse verse, turn-up makes it obvious on earbuds.

CoProducer reports noise floor beside LUFS. Use it when the arrangement has long gaps.

What auto-repair will try

A cautious high-pass may appear when rumble or DC evidence is strong. It will not invent a denoiser for air hiss. That is still mix or restoration work.

If the floor is the story, clean the session before you chase −14.

Upload a quiet bounce to Analyze and read noise floor next to LUFS before you ship.
Is −14 always louder than −16 after normalization?
Not in the way people mean. Playback gain aims for a similar lane; crest and spectrum still decide punch.
Should I raise the master to avoid turn-up?
Raise intentional signal, not noise. Fix the floor first.
