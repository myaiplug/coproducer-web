Crest factor is not loudness

NoDAW Labs · Lacquer Vol. 02

Crest factor describes peak-to-RMS height. LUFS describes perceived loudness over time. You can raise crest and still miss −14 LUFS, or hit −14 LUFS with a crushed crest. They answer different questions.

Two numbers, two jobs

Integrated LUFS is what stores use to decide playback gain. Crest (and the related dynamic-range readouts in CoProducer) tell you whether the mix still has punch after you chase that lane. Treating them as synonyms is how people print a flat −14 and call it musical.

On Drippin, the repair moved LUFS from −11.92 toward −14.12 while clip count fell from 231,310 to 0. The crest story changed because overs were no longer glued to full scale — not because the arrangement grew taller.

A faster diagnostic

If LUFS is hot and crest is already low, do not add another bus limiter. Take gain out. If LUFS is quiet and crest is huge, you may still be fine for streaming — quiet files can be turned up, which then reveals noise floor problems.

Measure both before you open a new plugin. That order saves the hour you would spend “making it slap” into a turn-down.

Is higher crest always better?
No. Extreme crest can mean unused headroom or a mix that will jump when normalized. Aim for intentional dynamics inside a legal loudness lane.
