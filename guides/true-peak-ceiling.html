True Peak & Inter-Sample Clipping

Published June 2026 · 6 min read

When digital audio is converted back to analog signals by digital-to-analog converters (DACs) or compressed into lossy codecs like AAC, MP3, or Opus, inter-sample peaks can exceed 0 dBFS even if sample-level peaks report no digital clipping.

Key Principle: Sample peak measures individual PCM sample values. True Peak (dBTP) reconstructs the continuous analog waveform between samples using oversampling, catching hidden distortion before it hits streaming codecs.

Why True Peak Matters for Streaming

Major streaming services compress your audio into lossy distribution formats. If your true peak exceeds their recommended ceiling (typically -1.0 dBTP), the lossy encoder will clip the reconstruction waveform, introducing harsh digital distortion (clicks, fuzz, and pumping).
