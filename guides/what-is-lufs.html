What is LUFS?
The complete guide

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

If you've ever uploaded a song to Spotify and thought "why is it quieter than everything else?" — you've experienced LUFS in action.

LUFS stands for Loudness Units relative to Full Scale. It is the international standard (ITU-R BS.1770) for measuring how loud audio sounds to the human ear. Unlike peak level (dBFS), LUFS accounts for how our ears perceive loudness across different frequencies.

Why LUFS matters

Every major streaming platform uses LUFS to normalize playback:

  • Spotify targets −14 LUFS
  • Apple Music targets −16 LUFS (Sound Check)
  • YouTube targets −14 LUFS
  • Amazon Music targets −14 LUFS (−2 dBTP peak)
  • TIDAL targets −14 LUFS

A hotter master is not played louder. It is turned down until it matches the lane. That is why chasing −8 LUFS usually wastes dynamics without winning volume on the store.
